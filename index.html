OPTIMIZING THE CO-COMPOSTING OF FISH BY-PRODUCTS WITH BIOCHAR-ENRICHED AGRICULTURAL RESIDUES: PROCESS DYNAMICS AND NUTRIENT TRANSFORMATION

Authors: Ngoc Do Tran Khanh*, Khanh Nguyen Thi Minh*, Khoa Ngo Bao*, Tam Le Huynh Minh*
Scientific Mentors: MSc. Hue Vo Thi Thuy**, MSc. Quang Nguyen Minh**, MSc Student. Anh Pham Thi Quynh*
*VNU-HCM High School for the Gifted (PTNK) · **Institute of Biotechnology and Environment, Nong Lam University HCMC

📋 Abstract

At many small-scale local markets, fish by-products are often disposed of using inefficient and unscientific methods such as unsanitary landfilling, open burning, or improper composting. These practices release NH₃ and H₂S, which negatively affect community life, as well as CH₄, a greenhouse gas that contributes to climate change. Therefore, various methods have been developed to utilize fish by-products, such as processing them into collagen, thermal processing to produce fish oil, or fish meal. This study aims to evaluate the effectiveness of using a microbial consortium consisting of Trichoderma sp. fungi, Bacillus sp. bacteria, and Streptomyces sp. actinomycetes to compost fish by-products into organic fertilizer. The composting process was carried out with a substrate composed of coconut coir, rice husk ash, and biochar, mixed with fish by-products at a 1:1 ratio. The monitored parameters included temperature, odor, moisture, porosity, pH, C/N ratio, and nutrient composition, analyzed using Kjeldahl, MBM, and FAES methods. The outcome is that a 50% substrate and 50% fish by-product mixture will produce compost with an optimal C/N ratio and aeration for plant growth. The significance of this study lies in identifying the most suitable substrate-to-by-product ratio in combination with specialized microbial groups to compost fish by-products into organic fertilizer. From this, a standardized formula can be developed to create convenient composting kits, contributing to sustainable agriculture.

🌱 1. Introduction & Background

Compost is the product of biological decomposition of organic matter under controlled thermophilic conditions, designed to eliminate pathogens, weed seeds, and produce organic fertilizer that meets soil application standards (Haug, 1993). In sustainable agriculture, compost improves soil structure, increases fertility, and maintains beneficial microbial communities, while reducing dependence on synthetic chemical fertilizers.

In Vietnam, where approximately 60,000 tons of household waste are generated daily, small-market fish waste causes severe odor and runoff issues. Incorporating synergistic microbial strains—Trichoderma sp., Bacillus sp., and Streptomyces sp.—accelerates decomposition, generates sufficient thermophilic heat to eliminate pathogens, and serves as biological antagonists against harmful soil pathogens. Structural substrates (coconut coir, rice husk ash, and biochar) provide vital aeration, retain moisture, and sustain microbial populations.

🔬 2. Research Hypothesis & Biological Rationale

It was hypothesized that Treatment 4 (NT4), characterized by a balanced 1:1 ratio of fish by-products to biochar-enriched agricultural residues, would exhibit the highest composting efficiency and yield a superior quality organic fertilizer compared to other treatments.

A 1:1 ratio provides sufficient structural carbon to offset the rapid nitrogen release from fish viscera, maintaining a favorable C:N balance for microbial metabolism. Physically, the biochar and coir matrix prevents compaction and waterlogging, avoiding anaerobic odor generation and facilitating an energetic thermophilic phase (>60°C).

🧪 3. Materials, Methods & Experimental Design

Fish by-products (heads, bones, viscera) were collected from An Dong, Cay Sot, and An Suong markets over 5 days, maintained under cold chain transport, and homogenized at the Nong Lam University Experimental Farm. Substrate consisted of coir, rice husk ash, and biochar (45:45:10 ratio) supplemented with 0.3% microbial inoculant. Five treatments of 8kg each were incubated in cloth-covered containers with continuous temperature and mass monitoring.

Treatment Fish Waste (%) Substrate (%) Mass (kg) Key Observations
NT1 (Control) 100% 0% 8.0 kg Dense, leachate leakage, low temp (28–35°C), anaerobic odor
NT2 90% 10% 8.0 kg Moderate compaction, leachate present, temp 35–45°C
NT3 70% 30% 8.0 kg Adequate aeration, high heat (50–60°C), no leachate

NT5 30% 70% 8.0 kg Over-aerated, moisture deficit, low temp (28–35°C)

📈 4. Results & Process Dynamics

Thermal monitoring from July 18 to August 21 demonstrated distinct phases: Treatment 4 (NT4) rapidly achieved temperatures exceeding 60°C, sustaining thermophilic pathogen eradication before stabilizing at ambient 35°C by hour 770 (Day 31). Leachate was strictly confined to NT1 and NT2, confirming that a 50% substrate inclusion provides complete moisture absorption and porosity.

🎯 5. Conclusion & Practical Implementation

Treatment 4 (50% fish by-products, 50% biochar-enriched agricultural residue substrate with 0.3% Trichoderma–Bacillus–Streptomyces inoculum) is proven optimal for rapid, odor-free bioconversion. Composting was complete by hour 770 under aerobic management with 6-day turn cycles. This provides a validated formula for modular home and market composting units in coastal and urban Vietnam.

SMART EARPHONES INTEGRATING PHYSIOLOGICAL MONITORING AND AUDIO PLAYBACK

Authors: Sy Duong-Quy1, Tuan Anh Nguyen1, Thi Thao Tram Tang1, Toi Van Nguyen1, Ha-To-Nhu Ong2, Khanh Ngoc Do Tran2*, Bao Khoa Ngo2, Tuan Dat Ho2, Quynh Anh Pham Thi2
Affiliations: 1Vietnam Society of Sleep Medicine (VSSM) · 2VNU-HCM High School for the Gifted (PTNK)
*Co-Author & Lead Youth Researcher / Presenter at 1st Vietnam-France Sleep Medicine Conference

📋 Abstract

This study presents the design of smart earphones integrating physiological monitoring and sleep-support functions. Optical sensors positioned at both ears are combined with a microcontroller to collect heart rate, blood oxygen saturation, and temperature in real time, while audio is transmitted through a separate Bluetooth channel to maintain listening quality. The system establishes physiological-state recognition based on an individually calibrated baseline heart rate and automatically controls music playback and pausing according to the transition between wakefulness and sleep. The paper describes the hardware architecture, signal-processing principles, and control mechanism of the system, and discusses current limitations and a future direction for integrating artificial intelligence to personalize sleep-supporting audio content based on users' physiological data.

🧠 I. Introduction and Background

1.1. Importance of Sleep and Digital Technology: Sleep is not merely a state of rest but a complex physiological process that plays an essential role in physical recovery, maintenance of brain function, and regulation of physiological functions. Sleep quality is associated with multiple aspects of health, including cognitive function, memory consolidation, emotional regulation, and physical health. As digital technology has become increasingly prevalent, screen-emitted blue light suppresses melatonin production, circadian regulation, and sleepiness.

1.2. Limitations of Wearable Devices for Sleep Support: Although smartwatches, rings, and headbands collect useful physiological data, most focus primarily on data collection and do not inherently integrate sleep-support audio playback. Users who desire physiological monitoring while listening to relaxing audio must wear two separate devices. Moreover, specialized solutions are often cumbersome and expensive.

1.3. Proposed Solution: This project proposes earphones integrating sleep-supporting audio playback, smartphone Bluetooth connectivity, and dual-ear optical biosensing within a single, compact, and affordable wearable form factor.

⚡ II. Hardware Architecture and Technical Principles

2.1. Overall Block Diagram: Organized into three main functional blocks: the physiological-sensing block (dual MAX30102 sensors), the central-processing block (ESP32 microcontroller), and the audio block (VHM-314 Bluetooth Audio Receiver with stereo speakers). BLE transmits telemetry while Bluetooth Classic (A2DP) handles high-fidelity audio.

2.2. Multi-Parameter Sensing Block: Dual MAX30102 optical sensors connected via I²C measure heart rate (HR), blood oxygen saturation (SpO₂), and temperature sequentially. The ESP32 processes raw PPG data and broadcasts via BLE.

2.3. Power Management & Clean Power-Rail Design: Separates power supplies into 1.8V for the analog MAX30102 core and 3.3V for LEDs and digital I²C pull-ups using ultra-low-noise LDO regulators (LD1117DT33CTR & LP3986-18B3F). LED currents are adaptively calibrated (7.2 mA to 15 mA) to match individual ear-tissue thickness, preventing thermal accumulation while maintaining high signal-to-noise ratio.

2.4. Data Communication & Hardware Interrupts: Uses a 32-sample FIFO buffer with continuous burst read at 100 Hz sampling rate, reducing microcontroller interrupts while sustaining reliable latency (~320 ms) suited for sleep staging.

📡 III. Data Processing & Transmission Algorithms

3.1. FIFO Data Buffering: The circular 32-sample FIFO buffer stores 6 bytes per sample (3 bytes Red, 3 bytes IR) and triggers a physical interrupt line (INT) when full, ensuring zero sample drop without saturating the ESP32 CPU.

3.2. Dual Bluetooth Streaming: BLE handles telemetry for ultra-low power consumption (~400 mAh battery lasting multi-hour overnight sessions), while Bluetooth Classic (A2DP) streams stereo audio with sub-millisecond phase synchronization required for effective binaural beat perception.

⚙️ IV. Control Logic in the Mobile Application

4.1. Core Application Logic: Manages sequential sensing (20s HR → 10s SpO₂ → 1s Temp = 31s per ear cycle). Integrates Dynamic Binaural Beats transitioning from Alpha (8–12 Hz) to Theta (4–8 Hz) to facilitate N1 sleep onset.

4.2. Baseline Heart-Rate Calibration: Rather than arbitrary static thresholds, the system computes an individualized baseline HR averaged over a 10-minute resting calibration window after removing artifacts (HR=0 or <30 bpm).

Closed-Loop Music Control Thresholds (Table 4.2.3):
  • Music-Pause Threshold: Current HR ≤ 85% of baseline (indicates transition to deep relaxation/sleep).
  • Music-Resume Threshold: Current HR ≥ 92.5% of baseline (indicates wakefulness or arousal).
  • Example: Baseline = 70 bpm → Pause at ≤ 59.5 bpm; Resume at ≥ 64.75 bpm.

4.4. Real-World Error & Noise Handling: Ignores motion artifacts and sudden drops (e.g., temperature falling from ~33°C to 26°C identifies earphone removal rather than medical hypothermia). Auto-pauses audio control until stable contact is re-established.

📱 V. User Interface, Applications & Practical Impact

Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI): Integrates standard clinical survey screening, real-time PPG time-series waveforms, and non-invasive ear-canal hearable monitoring for everyday domestic sleep support.

🔮 VI. Limitations & Future AI Entrainment Direction

Future work explores lightweight on-device AI models that dynamically modulate binaural difference frequencies (from Alpha 8–12 Hz down to Delta 0.5–4 Hz) in real-time response to autonomic nervous system shifts, tailoring restorative auditory stimulation to each individual's neuro-acoustic sensitivity.

EFFECTS OF LOW FREQUENCY BINAURAL BEATS ON SLEEP ARCHITECTURE IN STUDENTS AT LAM DONG MEDICAL COLLEGE

Authors: Sy Duong-Quy1, Tuan Anh Nguyen1, Thi Thao Tram Tang1, Toi Van Nguyen1, Ha-To-Nhu Ong2, Khanh Ngoc Do Tran2*, Bao Khoa Ngo2, Tuan Dat Ho2, Quynh Anh Pham Thi2, Ngoc Luu-Bich, Dung Nguyen-Thi-Thu, Thai Nguyen-Duy, Vinh Nguyen-Nhu, Thomas Penzel
Affiliations: 1Vietnam Society of Sleep Medicine (VSSM) · 2VNU-HCM High School for the Gifted (PTNK)
*Co-First Author & Clinical Data Analyst · Lam Dong Medical College Research Project

📋 Abstract

Objective: To evaluate the acute effects of low-frequency Binaural Beats (BB) at 2 Hz delta frequency on sleep architecture in students at Lam Dong Medical College.

Methods: A cross-sectional intervention study was conducted on 35 students (12 males, 23 females, aged 18-25). Each participant underwent three consecutive nights: Night 1 (no music), Night 2 (regular music), and Night 3 (BB delta 2 Hz for 30 minutes before bedtime). Sleep architecture was measured using Frenz smart wristbands. Data were analyzed using Repeated Measures ANOVA and paired t-tests with Bonferroni correction.

Results: Baseline deep sleep (NREM3) was remarkably low (31.2 ± 22.6 minutes, 8.9% of total sleep time). BB delta 2 Hz increased deep sleep by +34.9 minutes (+112%, p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.98) and REM sleep by +13.9 minutes (+67%, p < 0.001, d = 0.85) compared to the no-music night. BB significantly outperformed regular music for both deep sleep (+18.5 minutes, p = 0.005) and REM (+8.1 minutes, p = 0.028). BMI was negatively correlated with the improvement in deep sleep (r = -0.34, p = 0.037); gender showed no effect (p = 0.475).

Conclusion: BB delta 2 Hz is a low-cost, non-pharmacological intervention with significant acute efficacy in improving deep sleep and REM sleep in medical students.

🧠 1. Introduction

Sleep plays an essential role in maintaining physical and mental health, particularly for healthcare students. Studies in Vietnam indicate that 44% to 75.9% of medical students suffer from poor sleep quality. Binaural Beats (BB) in the delta frequency (0.5–4 Hz) facilitate brainwave entrainment into slow-wave restorative sleep. This study provides the first objective evaluation of 2 Hz delta BB on sleep macro-architecture among medical students in Vietnam.

🔬 2. Materials and Methods

2.1 Crossover Study Design: 35 students (12 males, 23 females, mean BMI 21.51 ± 2.06 kg/m²) underwent 3 consecutive nights: Night 1 (no music baseline), Night 2 (regular music active control for 30 min), and Night 3 (2 Hz delta BB primary intervention for 30 min).

2.3 FRENZ® Brainband Monitoring: Sleep architecture was objectively recorded using the FDA-registered FRENZ® Brainband incorporating 7 dry EEG/EMG electrodes, PPG pulse oximetry, and 3-axis motion accelerometry, providing high concordance with gold-standard polysomnography (PSG).

2.4 Pitch-Shifted Audio Intervention: Implemented a pitch-shifting technique on original instrumental music to create a constant 2 Hz frequency differential between left and right channels without raw sine-wave dissonance.

📊 3. Primary Quantitative Findings

Key Sleep Macro-Architecture Restructuring (35 Participants):
  • Baseline (Night 1): Deep sleep (NREM3) was severely depleted at 31.2 ± 22.6 mins (8.9% TST). 11.4% of students had 0 deep sleep.
  • 2 Hz Delta BB (Night 3): Increased deep sleep to 66.0 ± 37.4 mins (+112% increase, +34.9 mins vs Night 1, p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.98).
  • REM Sleep Expansion: Increased REM sleep from 20.9 to 34.8 mins (+67% increase, +13.9 mins, p < 0.001, d = 0.85).
  • BB vs Regular Music: 2 Hz Delta BB significantly outperformed regular music for deep sleep (+18.5 mins, p = 0.005) and REM sleep (+8.1 mins, p = 0.028).
  • BMI Correlation: Normal weight students achieved significantly greater deep sleep gains (+38.7 mins) than overweight students (+24.5 mins, p = 0.037).

🎯 4. Discussion & Conclusion

2 Hz delta Binaural Beats represent a highly effective, safe, non-pharmacological intervention for counteracting sleep fragmentation, enhancing NREM3 slow-wave physical recovery and REM cognitive consolidation among high-stress medical students.
